About me

I hold a degree in telecommunications engineering from the Polytechnic University of Madrid and a PhD from the Polytechnic University of Valencia. I joined the University of Castilla-La Mancha in 1999 and have been an associate professor at the Polytechnic School of Cuenca since 2010, where I teach quality and management and health technology assessment, clinical engineering and electronic instrumentation at undergraduate and master’s level. I have supervised final-year projects, co-supervised a doctoral thesis and taken part in teaching innovation projects.

My career began in biomedical engineering, applying electrocardiographic signal processing to atrial fibrillation and sudden cardiac death, and has extended to clinical engineering and electromedicine. Today I take part in projects on artificial intelligence and wearable devices for preventing cryptogenic stroke. I have been national training coordinator of SEEIC, the Spanish Society of Clinical Engineering and Electromedicine, served on its Board until 2018 and have sat on its advisory council since 2024. I have worked with the Ministry of Education and the Public Employment Service on expert panels defining qualifications in clinical engineering.

In recent years my work has expanded to forest bioeconomy and territory. I have been principal investigator of UFIL Cuenca and Retos Territorio-Empresa, led forest entrepreneurship laboratories, authored Redeia’s white paper on forests, and I take part in FORESTED 2.0, a project on high-value timber for construction. I co-direct the Demographic Challenge Opportunities Chair and contribute to science outreach in rural areas.

From May 2019 to December 2020 I was Councillor for Entrepreneurship, Innovation and New Technologies at Cuenca City Council, and fourth deputy mayor. Since December 2020 I have been a vice-rector of UCLM, currently for Culture, Sport and Social Engagement. This website is personal and does not represent the University.

Interactive tool · 2026

MDR/IVDR medical device classifier

It guides you, question by question, through qualifying a product (whether it is a medical device and whether the MDR or the IVDR applies) and classifying its risk under the Annex VIII rules, with its EMDN category and conformity assessment route. Each result cites the article, rule and indent that determine it, and can be compared with the COM(2025) 1023 revision proposal.

  • 113 solved practice cases: qualification, MDR and IVDR
  • Copyable report with the full reasoning
  • Rules published as data (JSON), open to review and reuse
  • Runs in the browser, with no sign-up or cookies

Course: Quality and management in healthcare technology, Degree in Biomedical Engineering. Teaching prototype under testing (regulations checked on 14/10/2026). For teaching and guidance only: it does not replace the manufacturer’s classification. The tool is in Spanish.

NewInteractive tool · 2026

PICO evidence search builder

A six-step guide from a structured question to a reproducible search strategy: concepts, controlled vocabulary (MeSH, Emtree, CINAHL, DeCS) and free text, translated into each database’s syntax and documented with the PRISMA-S checklist. It builds the search strings; it does not run them or replace review by an information specialist.

  • PICO, diagnostic PICO, PECO, economic PICO and SPIDER frameworks
  • Syntax for PubMed, Embase, Cochrane, CINAHL, Scopus, Web of Science and BVS, plus trial registries
  • Five practice cases, including the proton therapy report and the transcatheter mitral valve
  • Runs in the browser, with no sign-up or cookies; progress is saved only in your browser

Course: Health Technology Assessment, Degree in Biomedical Engineering (Unit 3: reviewing scientific evidence). Teaching prototype under testing. For guidance only: syntax must be checked on each platform. The tool is in Spanish.

FeaturedLinkedIn post · 26 Oct 2026

España se prepara para la evaluación europea de tecnologías sanitarias

On 20 October, the Spanish Agency of Medicines and Medical Devices (AEMPS) presented the results of the European Commission's Technical Support Instrument (TSI) for implementing the EU HTA Regulation. It includes templates, integration of national and European procedures, training materials, and the participation of clinicians and patients, who are already involved in defining the national PICO (population, intervention, comparator, outcomes). It coincides with the selection of the first medical devices for joint clinical assessment.
